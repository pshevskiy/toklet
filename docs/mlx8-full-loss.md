# Frozen full-loss trial for the public eight-bit checkpoint

This experiment uses the immutable [eight-bit Qwen snapshot](mlx8-checkpoint.md), native Rust/Metal and the same Apple app coordinators already checked on [Mac and iPhone](mlx8-app-jobs.md). It evaluates the full frozen 10,001-token corpus as 10,000 scored targets on each device, resetting at the original 1,024-target block boundary with capacity 1,025 and chunk size one. Raw token IDs, target order, stop policy, model bytes and numerical settings remain unchanged throughout collection.

The admission gate compares mean negative log likelihood against the frozen FP32 baseline of 2.883725149929484 nats/token. An increase of at most 0.05 is required. Successful collection and validator checks are distinct from passing that quality gate. The selected public checkpoint's model card does not establish exact prequantization source-byte identity, so this comparison cannot isolate quantization effects from other source differences.

Both completed collections score all 10,000 targets with mean loss 2.884953696657687, an increase of 0.0012285467282029394 over the baseline. Both pass the unchanged 0.05 gate. Every paired greedy ID, target logit and negative log likelihood agrees exactly across devices. This establishes the selected candidate's full loss acceptance; full task quality and adoption remain separate.

The collector authenticates the existing Mac runner and native package, then collects a fresh tiny phone storage observation before copying and launching the signed eight-bit job. Each physical stage preserves exact device/app ownership, consumed-byte provenance, same-device storage admission and cleanup. Full validators replay both complete raw target streams with their respective installation scope. The publisher recomputes both means and quality decisions and retains per-target cross-device comparisons, native process peaks, power/thermal observations and private evidence hashes.

Observed native lifetime process peaks are 553,075,648 bytes on M1 Pro and 566,265,888 bytes on iPhone 14 Pro. Low Power Mode is off throughout both streams. Observed thermal states are zero on Mac and zero/one/two on phone. These conditions are retained with every raw sample; this accuracy collection does not qualify representative or sustained performance.

All seven previously passing portable commands (101 methods), five owned collection commands and 45 transport commands are bound. Transport comprises 41 devicectl operations and four host boot queries. Eight cleanup endpoints finish absent: seven natural absences and one verified initial exact-app preflight reset before copying or inference. No primary, cleanup or endpoint errors remain. Both complete validators replay successfully from the exact consumed bytes; aggregation and gate decisions are independently recomputed.

Evidence: [qualification](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-22-native-mlx8-full-loss-v1.json), [raw inventory](../benchmarks/results/2026-10-22-native-mlx8-full-loss-raw-v1.json). The guarded publisher retains 108 raw files and 73 source copies, protects consumed/source/private bindings through exclusive publication, and keeps device identifiers in local hash-bound private logs. Numerical sources, frozen gates, compiled apps and protected historical CPU binaries remain unchanged.

The [prior four-bit rejection](mlx-full-loss.md) and [upstream diagnosis](mlx-upstream-loss.md) remain retained. Neither another candidate nor successful execution changes those decisions. Full 100-task quality, representative and sustained performance, physical state/lifecycle, downloaded chat-template compatibility and wider architecture/discovery qualification remain separate work.

Astra accepted selected-checkpoint full loss quality after independently replaying both 10,000-target streams and verifying 262 pins, 108 raw files, 73 source copies, every paired target, command/test counts, storage and exact-app reset evidence. Privacy, 234 links and whitespace checks pass.
